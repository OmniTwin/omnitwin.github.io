# Regulated processes that require a documented human approval, where AI agents now act, and where small firms buy self-serve. Status as of 16 October 2026

Question tested: the founder's thesis is "audit and compliance will always require human approvals for controlled systems." For each controlled process this note asks five things. Does a rule or contract force a named human to approve, and leave a record, before the action? Are AI agents drafting or doing that action now? Is there pain or enforcement? What do small firms buy today, at what price, and can they buy it self-serve? Would a generic gate fit (routing to a named human, a sealed tamper-evident receipt, a PII-tokenising vault), and what obligations would the vendor take on?

**Method and source quality.** In this session WebFetch could not resolve any host. finra.org, ecfr.gov, sec.gov, debevoise.com, mayerbrown.com, hklaw.com and getfreed.ai all failed with ENOTFOUND. The sandbox proxy also refused curl CONNECT (403) to every regulator domain tried. **So every finding below comes from search-engine result snippets and their summaries, not from pages I opened.** Where a snippet quotes primary text (FINRA's own rule page or Regulatory Notice page, the FCA Handbook, govinfo for 21 CFR 11, artificialintelligenceact.eu, legislation.gov.uk, the NY State Comptroller, the IRS OPR alert), that is noted. Some statements come from my own knowledge of the rule text and could not be checked this session. They are marked **[knowledge, unverified this session]**. I used 61 web searches.

## 1. Financial promotions and communications (FINRA 2210, SEC Marketing Rule, FCA s21/COBS 4, 17a-4)

### Takeaway
This is the clearest existing per-item human-approval mandate: a FINRA registered principal must approve every retail communication before use, and the approver's name and the approval date must be kept as a record. **But on 9 July 2026 FINRA proposed (Regulatory Notice 26-14) to replace that mandatory per-item approval with risk-based supervision. It named AI-generated content and social media volume as the reason.** This is a direct counter-example to "will always require." The regulator with the most explicit approval mandate is moving toward "approve the high-risk items, sample and surveil the rest." The SEC Marketing Rule for RIAs never required pre-approval; it requires policies plus retained copies. UK s21 approvers and COBS 4.11 do require approval records. The tools buyers use (Red Oak, Saifr, Hadrius, Luthor, Smarsh, Global Relay) are almost all sold through sales calls, and the archive is the regulated system of record.

### Cited findings
Rule text and record requirement
- FINRA Rule 2210(b)(1)(A), quoted in a snippet of FINRA's own rule page: "An appropriately qualified registered principal of the member must approve each retail communication before the earlier of its use or filing with FINRA's Advertising Regulation Department." A retail communication is any written communication distributed to more than 25 retail investors within 30 days. Correspondence is supervised under the correspondence rules and does not need pre-use approval. — [FINRA Rule 2210](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2210?page=1); [FINRA "What and When to File"](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/advertising/chart)
- 2210(b)(4) records **[knowledge, unverified this session]**: retail and institutional communications must be kept for the 17a-4(b) period in a format that complies with 17a-4. The record must include the communication and its dates of use, "the name of any registered principal who approved each retail communication and the date that approval was given," and, where nobody approved it before use, the name of whoever prepared or distributed it. New member firms must file retail communications 10 business days before first use during their first year (2210(c)(1)).
- 17a-4 after the 2022 amendments: firms may keep records in WORM format or under an "audit-trail alternative." That alternative must let the original be recreated after any change and must log the date, time and identity of each change. A "designated executive officer" may give the regulator-access undertaking in place of a third party. Third parties that keep their role had to file new undertakings with FINRA by 3 May 2023. Cloud providers may give an alternative undertaking. — [Global Relay summary](https://www.globalrelay.com/resources/blog/sec-brings-recordkeeping-into-the-21st-century-with-rule-17a-4-amendments/); [ACA summary](https://www.acaglobal.com/insights/amendments-rule-17a-4-electronic-recordkeeping-requirement/); [WilmerHale alert PDF](https://www.wilmerhale.com/-/media/files/shared_content/editorial/publications/wh_publications/client_alert_pdfs/20221129-sec-adopts-amendments-to-electronic-recordkeeping-requirements-for-broker-dealers-and-security-based-swap-dealers.pdf)
- UK COBS 4.11.1R (snippet of the FCA Handbook): "A firm must make an adequate record of any financial promotion: it communicates; it approves; or of which it confirms compliance." Records are kept indefinitely for pension transfer, conversion or opt-out promotions; six years for life policies and personal pensions; five years for MiFID business; three years otherwise. — [FCA Handbook COBS 4.11](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/COBS/4/11.html)
- The UK approver gateway, in effect since 7 February 2024, requires a specific FCA permission to approve promotions made by unauthorised persons. Firms approving their own promotions do not need gateway permission but must meet COBS 4. A secondary guide says approvers must record approver identity, date, content and basis. — [FD Capital s21 guide](https://www.fdcapital.co.uk/section-21-financial-promotions-guide/); [FD Capital gateway guide](https://www.fdcapital.co.uk/fca-financial-promotions-gateway-guide/)
- SEC Marketing Rule (206(4)-1) **[knowledge, unverified this session]**: the rule contains no pre-approval requirement. Rule 206(4)-7 requires written compliance policies, and Rule 204-2(a)(11) requires a copy of each advertisement to be kept. Pre-use review by the CCO is market practice, not a rule.

The 2026 change: FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-14
- The notice was issued 9 July 2026. It proposes to "replace the prescriptive principal pre-use approval requirement" with risk-based standards under which firms adopt written procedures to decide which retail communications need pre-use approval. Lower-risk content could be handled by surveillance, sampling and post-use review. FINRA said the requirement "presents significant challenges ... particularly communications on social media and some generated by AI" because of speed and volume. It is still a proposal: comments closed 11 September 2026, and it needs FINRA Board approval and an SEC filing. — [FINRA RN 26-14](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-14); [RN 26-14 PDF](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-07/Regulatory-Notice-26-14.pdf); [Mondaq summary](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/financial-services/1818992/finra-proposes-amendments-to-rule-2210-focusing-on-modernizing-supervision-and-review-of-retail-communications-to-address-evolving-communication-practices-and-technologies-such-as-ai); [Altswire](https://altswire.com/finra-proposes-ending-mandatory-pre-use-review-of-retail-communications/); [Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2401/2026-07-14-pivotal-moment-broker-dealer-communications-finra)
- FINRA's position is that its rules are "technologically neutral," that a firm stays responsible whether a person or an AI wrote the communication, and that AI output may be correspondence, institutional or retail depending on content and distribution. — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/financial-services/1815772/finra-proposes-risk-based-overhaul-of-rule-2210-communications-review)
- Comment letters, per snippets: NASAA (11 September 2026) wants "more definite baseline expectations." An investor clinic proposes a regulatory floor of high-risk categories that keep mandatory approval. FSI asks FINRA to say whether the framework covers AI content and to extend the risk-based approach to recordkeeping. The Committee of Annuity Insurers supports it. One report counted 28 letters. — [NASAA letter](https://www.nasaa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/NASAA-Comment-Letter-re-FINRA-Reg-Notice-26-14_09-11-2026.pdf); [FSI letter](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/NoticeComment/Financial%20Services%20Institute%20-%20FSI_Andrew%20Hartnett_26-14_9.11.2026.pdf); [CAI letter](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/NoticeComment/Committee%20of%20Annuity%20Insurers%20Comment%20Letter%20-%20Regulatory%20Notice%2026-14.pdf)

- AI notetakers for advisers (Jump, Zocks) draft client follow-up emails. Jump reportedly archives to Smarsh, Global Relay or Hadrius. In September 2026 Zocks and Archive Intel launched transcript screening and archiving for $12 per user per month. Jump and Zocks are reported at roughly $100–150 per advisor per month (sources conflict). — [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/fintech/archive-intel-zocks-pair-up-to-tackle-ai-notetaker-compliance-gap/268050); [layer3labs comparison](https://www.layer3labs.io/comparisons/jump-ai-alternatives)

### Inferences
- The founder's sealed receipt ("principal X approved communication hash H at time T") is almost literally the 2210(b)(4) record. Mechanically this is the best fit of any area. Commercially it sits between two entrenched categories: AI rule checkers (Saifr, Hadrius, Red Oak) and 17a-4 archives (Smarsh, Global Relay). Both sell through sales calls and via compliance consultants, not by card.
- If 26-14 is adopted, the per-item mandate becomes "approve what your written procedures call high-risk." That shrinks the mandated volume, but it creates new demand for evidence of risk-tiering: which items were routed to a principal, which were sampled, and why. A gate with policy-based routing and sealed records for both approved and auto-passed items fits that world better than a pure "approve everything" product. Timing risk is high, because the final rule shape is unknown and probably 2027 or later.
- Vault fit is weak for marketing, where approvers must read the real copy and there is little PII. It is moderate for AI-drafted client emails, which are correspondence under 3110(b) with no pre-approval mandate.
- Vendor obligations: if the receipt store is the firm's official 2210(b)(4) record, it must meet 17a-4(f) (WORM or audit-trail alternative). It also needs either a third-party undertaking filed with FINRA or the firm's DEO accepting responsibility. Under the 2024 Reg S-P amendments, firms must oversee service providers, who must give breach notice within 72 hours; smaller entities had to comply by 3 June 2026 **[knowledge, unverified this session]**. A solo founder can avoid the 17a-4 role by staying a pre-archive step: export every sealed receipt into the firm's existing archive and never claim to be the system of record.

### Gaps
- I could not open the text of 2210, 17a-4 or 26-14. The 2210(b)(4) wording and the Reg S-P dates are from memory.
- Counts of small FINRA members and RIAs, and the share using AI for marketing, were not checked.
- I found no FCA guidance specific to AI-generated promotions.
- No self-serve price was found for any approval or archive tool except Archive Intel + Zocks at $12 per user per month.

## 2. Payments and finance operations (dual control, vendor bank-detail changes, AI AP agents)

### Takeaway
No US law requires a small business to have two people approve a payment. The pull comes from fraud losses (business email compromise, or BEC), plus contracts: bank security-procedure agreements, NACHA's 2026 fraud-monitoring rules, and crime and cyber insurance terms. AI agents are now coding, recommending approval for and paying bills inside Ramp, QuickBooks and BILL. Approval controls are tiered upward: QuickBooks native approvals are on Advanced only, BILL custom approval policies are on Corporate, and BILL dual control reportedly needs Enterprise. ApprovalMax already sells self-serve approvals with an audit trail for Xero and QuickBooks at roughly $50–110 per org per month. Fit and self-serve are strong, the mandate is weak, and the competition is real.

### Cited findings
Rules and contracts
- NACHA 2026 Risk Management rules: non-consumer Originators, Third-Party Service Providers and Third-Party Senders must have "risk-based processes and procedures that are reasonably intended to identify entries that are suspected of being unauthorized or sent under false pretenses," and must review them at least once a year. Every transaction need not be screened. "False pretenses" covers BEC, vendor impersonation and payroll impersonation. Phase 1 started 20 March 2026 for those originating 6 million or more entries in 2023. Phase 2, for all others, took effect 22 June 2026 (19 June was a holiday). — [Nacha phase 2](https://www.nacha.org/rules/risk-management-topics-fraud-monitoring-phase-2); [Nacha phase 1](https://www.nacha.org/rules/risk-management-topics-fraud-monitoring-phase-1); [Kaufman Rossin](https://kaufmanrossin.com/blog/2026-nacha-rule-changes/)
- Insurance: crime and cyber "social engineering" and funds-transfer-fraud cover is narrow and often denied depending on who gave the instruction and how it was verified. A law-firm note warns that replying to the same email thread may not count as verification. I found no source confirming that a callback is a standard condition of coverage. — [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/social-engineering-fraud-and-your-crime-policy-why-your-insurer-may-deny-claim-and); [Lowenstein](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/call-me-maybe-the-stealth-disappearance-of-social-engineering-and-fraudulent-instruction-coverage-insurance)
- UCC Article 4A "commercially reasonable security procedures" and bank treasury agreements that require dual control on wires and ACH **[knowledge, unverified this session]**.

AI agents in the process
- Ramp "Agents for AP" (7 October 2025) code invoices, check for fraud, "recommend approvals" with a summary of vendor history and contracts, and complete card payments; Ramp says "You stay in control." Auto-coding and approval recommendations need Ramp Plus. — [Ramp announcement](https://ramp.com/blog/ramp-ap-agents-announcement); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ramp-launches-agents-for-ap-to-automate-accounts-payable-302576975.html)
- QuickBooks: an Accounting Agent and Payments Agent sit in lower tiers, with Finance Agent and Workflows on Advanced. Native bill approval is reportedly Advanced-only, with "no separate approval record built for audit." Bill Pay Elite with approval workflows is reportedly bundled into Advanced from 1 August 2026. Prices conflict: Advanced is quoted at $275 or $340 per month. — [ApprovalMax blog (competitor)](https://blog.approvalmax.com/accounts-payable-automation-for-quickbooks); [usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/quickbooks-ai/); [Factura](https://factura.ai/quickbooks-bill-pay-pricing/); [ApprovalMax on QBO limits](https://blog.approvalmax.com/quickbooks-bill-approval-workflows-limits-better-controls)
- BILL: Essentials, Team and Corporate are quoted at $45/$55/$79 or $49/$65/$89 per user per month (sources conflict). Custom approval policies are on Corporate; "Enterprise adds single sign-on and dual control." — [Capterra BILL](https://www.capterra.com/p/166559/BILL/pricing/); [dupple review](https://dupple.com/reviews/bill); [BILL pricing](https://bill.com/pricing)
- Agentic payments standards are building signed-approval objects themselves. Google AP2 (16 September 2025) uses Intent, Cart and Payment "mandates" as signed W3C Verifiable Credentials. Visa has the Trusted Agent Protocol and Mastercard has Agent Pay with agentic tokens. These are consumer-commerce rails, not SMB AP. Source quality is low (vendor blogs). — [eco.com AP2](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15192002-ap2-protocol-explained-google-s-agentic-commerce-standard-2026); [eco.com Visa vs Mastercard](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15192003-mastercard-agent-pay-vs-visa-trusted-agent-2026-compared)

Pain
- IC3 2025: BEC accounted for $3.046B in reported losses across 24,768 complaints, of which over $30M had a confirmed AI nexus. Total cyber-enabled losses were $20.877B. These figures come from secondary coverage of the report. — [McDonald Hopkins](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/the-sobering-truth-of-the-fbis-2025-internet-crime-complaint-center-report); [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/cyber/business-email-fraud-drives-bulk-of-us-cybercrime-losses-analysis-590687.aspx)
- The AFP 2025 survey, via Relish, found 79% of organisations had a payment fraud attempt in 2024. BEC and vendor impersonation were the top methods. — [Relish](https://relishiq.com/blog/supplier-bank-account-verification-tools/)
- In Resilience's claims book, payment-transfer fraud rose from 2.9% to 9.2% of insured losses over two years. — [CFO Dive](https://www.cfodive.com/news/resilience-study-ties-human-error-85percent-cyber-losses/826909/)

What small firms buy
- ApprovalMax for Xero and QuickBooks: legacy flat price of $59.40 per month, or tiers of Standard $49.50, Advanced about $79–95 and Premium about $111 per org per month. Standard includes "unlimited approval workflows, unlimited users ... full audit trail & fraud prevention," and the product page says it catches bank-detail changes. It is moving to usage-based pricing, sized by approver count and document volume, from 3 August 2026, starting in the UK. Self-serve with a free trial. — [ApprovalMax pricing](https://landing.approvalmax.com/pricing); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/176713/ApprovalMax/pricing/); [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/approvalmax); [ApprovalMax pricing change](https://blog.approvalmax.com/weve-changed-the-way-we-charge); [ApprovalMax QBO page](https://www.approvalmax.com/quickbooks-online)
- No public small-business pricing was found for vendor bank-account verification tools such as Eftsure or Trustpair. — [Relish guide](https://relishiq.com/blog/supplier-bank-account-verification-tools/)

### Inferences
- This is the only area where all of the following hold: small buyers pay by card, AI agents already initiate the regulated action (paying money), and a missing control is visibly expensive. The control the founder's assets express best is an out-of-band named approver plus a phone callback before (a) any change to vendor bank details and (b) any agent-initiated payment above a threshold. That is the standard anti-BEC control.
- The pull is not a legal mandate. The NACHA rules require risk-based monitoring, not per-payment approval. Selling "compliance" would overclaim; selling "fraud loss prevention, plus evidence for your bank, insurer or auditor" is accurate.
- Competition: ApprovalMax already owns "approval layer with audit trail for SMB accounting" at about $50–110 per month. Platforms are pulling approvals into their own tiers (QBO Advanced bundling Bill Pay Elite; Ramp agents with built-in approval). The opening is cross-platform agent actions (one gate across Ramp, QBO, Stripe and banks) and an approval channel that is not email (phone and Slack), where email-thread BEC is the attack.
- Vault fit is mixed. Tokenising bank details hides them from the model. For a bank-detail change, though, the approver must compare the real number with what the vendor says on the callback. Show the last four digits plus a verified-match flag, not a token.
- Vendor obligations are light if the founder never moves money (no money-transmitter licence). Customers will expect SOC 2, and holding raw bank details invites GLBA-style security questionnaires.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm the BILL "dual control" tier or the QBO plan gating from Intuit's or BILL's own pages.
- I found no Reddit or forum evidence of SMBs asking for more approval control than they have.
- Insurer callback conditions were not confirmed from a policy form.

## 3. Healthcare (clinician sign-off on AI notes and messages, prior auth, HIPAA)

### Takeaway
Rules do require human sign-off. Medicare requires the clinician to authenticate AI-transcribed notes. California AB 3030 drops its AI disclaimer if a provider reviewed the message. Texas SB 1188 requires practitioners to review AI-created records. About 11 states ban AI-only prior-authorization denials, though those laws bind payers. AI presence is very high. But the sign-off happens inside the EHR or scribe product, which is already the system of record. Handling PHI, including a vault holding it, makes the vendor a HIPAA business associate. This is a poor fit for a solo founder avoiding compliance obligations.

### Cited findings
- CMS clarified in September 2025 that a physician or NPP must authenticate records transcribed by another party, "including artificial intelligence or dictation software." First Coast says the provider signs and the AI scribe need not. — [LeadingAge](https://leadingage.org/cms-clarifies-medicare-signature-requirements/); [First Coast](https://medicare.fcso.com/evaluation-and-management/scribe-services); [Noridian](https://med.noridianmedicare.com/web/jadme/topics/documentation/signature-requirements)
- California AB 3030 (effective 1 January 2025) requires an AI disclaimer and contact instructions on genAI patient clinical communications. Messages that a licensed provider has read and reviewed are exempt. There is no specific penalty; enforcement is through licensing boards. — [Hogan Lovells](https://ca.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/new-california-laws-impact-uses-of-ai-by-health-care-providers-insurers-and-vendors); [Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/california_passes_novel_law_governing_generative_ai_healthcare_1224.html)
- Texas SB 1188 (effective 1 September 2025): a practitioner using AI for diagnosis must "review all records created with AI" consistent with Texas Medical Board standards and must disclose AI use. The same law requires EHRs to be stored in the US. — [Texas Medical Association](https://www.texmed.org/Template.aspx?id=66759); [Hall Render](https://hallrender.com/2025/08/19/new-texas-law-on-ehr-data-localization-and-ai/); [Akin](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/texas-enacts-a-pair-of-ai-governance-laws-hb-149-and-sb-1188)
- Prior authorization: about 11 states ban AI-only denials without physician review, including California SB 1120 and 2026 laws in Alabama, Colorado (HB 1139), Georgia, Iowa, Utah and Washington. Bill numbers conflict across sources. These laws bind health plans and utilisation-review organisations, not small practices. — [Becker's](https://www.beckerspayer.com/policy-updates/7-ai-health-insurance-state-laws-passed-in-2026/); [Rise Health](https://www.risehealth.org/insights-articles/article/ai-health-insurance-state-laws-2026/)
- AI presence: an AMA 2026 survey is cited at 81% of physicians using AI professionally; another source says 66%, so the figures conflict. More than 100 health systems use genAI for inbox drafting. A JAMA Network Open study (7 July 2026) found patients "strongly preferred clinician review" of AI-drafted portal messages. A Dartmouth analysis of 146,000 conversations found drafts can add editing work. — [ona.health](https://ona.health/blog/post/ai-medical-practice-statistics); [telehealth.org](https://telehealth.org/news/patients-accept-ai-drafted-portal-messages-but-only-with-clinician-review/); [Patient Care](https://www.patientcareonline.com/view/ai-drafts-for-patient-portal-replies-may-increase-primary-care-clinician-editing-burden)

## 5. Life sciences and GxP (21 CFR Part 11, EU Annex 11 and draft Annex 22)

### Takeaway
Part 11 is the closest conceptual match to "signed receipt of who approved exactly what and when." It requires the printed name, the date and time, and the meaning of each signature, with the signature linked to the record and covered by audit trails. Draft EU Annex 22 would keep genAI and LLMs out of critical GMP uses and require qualified human review elsewhere. But a compliant product needs validation, e-signature controls such as re-authentication with two components, and supplier audits. That makes it a different product with heavy obligations, sold to buyers who buy eQMS suites.

### Cited findings
- 21 CFR 11.50(a), from govinfo-derived text: signed electronic records must show "(1) The printed name of the signer; (2) The date and time when the signature was executed; and (3) The meaning (such as review, approval, responsibility, or authorship)." §11.70: signatures "shall be linked to their respective electronic records" so they cannot be excised, copied or transferred. — [govinfo CFR 11.10](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title21-vol1/pdf/CFR-2025-title21-vol1-sec11-10.pdf); [NIH FITBIR Part 11 table](https://fitbir.nih.gov/sites/fitbir/files/inline-files/21CFR11Compliance.pdf)
- From the rule, **[knowledge, unverified this session]**: §11.10(e) requires secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails. §11.10(a) requires validation. §11.100(c) requires the regulated firm to certify to FDA that it uses e-signatures. §11.200 requires non-biometric e-signatures to use at least two distinct identification components.
- Draft Annex 22 (PIC/S and EMA consultation, July 2025) covers static, deterministic models. GenAI and LLMs are excluded from critical GMP applications and allowed in non-critical ones if qualified staff confirm outputs, with operator responsibilities defined and records kept. About 5,600 comments came in, a workshop was held 30 June–1 July 2026, and it is not final. Revised Annex 11 had been expected in summer or September 2026, but I found no confirmation of adoption. — [ECA](https://www.gmp-compliance.org/gmp-news/what-is-human-in-the-loop); [ISPE](https://ispe.org/pharmaceutical-engineering/may-june-2026/innovation-and-regulation-danish-medicines-agency-insights); [PharmOut](https://www.pharmout.net/annex-22-artificial-intelligence/); [QbD Group](https://qbdgroup.com/en/blog/annex-11-revision-digital-compliance)
- FDA issued draft guidance on AI for regulatory decision-making in January 2025 and finalised its Computer Software Assurance guidance in September 2025, both per secondary sources. "Who signs the record when an AI agent runs the analysis" is described as unresolved. — [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/21-cfr-part-11-compliance-ai-systems); [Stacker](https://stacker.com/stories/ai/when-ai-agent-runs-analysis-who-signs-record)
- Small-biotech eQMS prices: Qualcy is $99 or $399 per month (sources conflict) with an IQ/OQ validation package. Synergia is $65 per user per month on AWS Marketplace. SimplerQMS lists $17,500 flat. Scilife is quote-only. — [SoftwareConnect](https://softwareconnect.com/reviews/qualcy-eqms/); [AWS Marketplace](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-f767k4wvh3eia); [Scilife pricing](https://www.scilife.io/pricing-qms)

### Inferences
- An approval tapped in Slack or Teams does not meet §11.200 without re-authentication at signing. The record must carry the signature's meaning, and the system must be validated, with change control on every release. That turns the generic gate into a validated GxP product.
- Small biotechs and CROs expect a supplier audit and a validation package from every GxP vendor. A solo founder shipping weekly cannot sustain that.
- The only realistic play is to stay out of the system of record: let an agent's action be approved through the gate, then push the decision into the customer's eQMS (Veeva, Qualio, Qualcy), where the Part 11 signature is applied. That sells to the eQMS vendor, not to small biotechs.

- I could not open the Part 11 text for §11.10(e), §11.100 or §11.200, the draft Annex 22 text, or FDA's January 2025 draft.

## Cross-cutting findings

1. **The thesis "audit and compliance will always require human approvals" is contradicted by 2026's direction of travel.** FINRA proposes dropping mandatory per-item approval because AI volume makes it unworkable (RN 26-14). The UK loosened Article 22 (DUAA, 5 February 2026). Colorado repealed and replaced its AI Act with a notice regime (SB 26-189). The EU delayed high-risk duties to December 2027. The CFPB withdrew its AI adverse-action guidance. Mandates are holding where a licensed person signs: clinicians, lawyers, tax practitioners, and Part 11 signatories. In those areas the signature already lives inside an incumbent system of record (EHR, court filing, e-file software, eQMS).
2. **What compliance increasingly asks for is defensible, risk-tiered supervision evidence**: which items a human approved, which were auto-passed under policy, and why. A gate that routes by risk and seals receipts for both approved and auto-passed items fits this better than "every action needs a human."
3. **Becoming the system of record is what creates vendor obligations**: 17a-4 undertakings, Part 11 validation, HIPAA BAAs, Reg S-P service-provider duties. A solo founder can stay outside them only by being a pre-step that exports sealed receipts into the customer's archive, EHR or eQMS. Even then, holding raw regulated data in the vault re-creates the obligations: HHS says encrypted PHI without the key still makes the host a business associate.
4. **The vault is an asset where reviewers don't need raw data** (HR, some client correspondence). It is a liability where they must check raw data (bank details, the exact marketing copy) or where hosting it triggers obligations (PHI, NPI).

### Bottom line
No area has all three of: a strong legal mandate for per-action human approval, card-paid small buyers, and light vendor obligations. The strongest mandates (FINRA 2210, Part 11, clinician authentication) come with heavy obligations or incumbent systems of record, and the most explicit of them is being relaxed because of AI. The best small-buyer fit (SMB payment controls) is pulled by fraud losses and contracts, not compliance law.

For a solo founder who wants to avoid compliance obligations, the defensible position is:
- (a) Sell SMB payment and bank-detail-change approvals as **fraud control with audit evidence**, not as compliance.
- (b) Keep receipts exportable into the customer's existing archive or system of record, so the founder never becomes the regulated record-keeper.
- (c) Revisit FINRA/RIA communications once RN 26-14 is final. A risk-tiered gate that seals evidence of both approved and auto-passed items could fit the new rule, sold through archive or compliance-vendor partnerships rather than by card.

The thesis should be restated as: "regulators increasingly require evidence of risk-based human supervision of AI actions." That is narrower than "always require human approvals," and much of it gets bundled into incumbents.
