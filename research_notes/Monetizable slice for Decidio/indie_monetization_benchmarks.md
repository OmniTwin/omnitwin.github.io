# Indie monetization benchmarks: solo founders selling direct in the AI coding-agent / AI-tool ecosystem (2025 to Oct 2026)

Method note: Only WebSearch was available in this session (direct page fetches to news.ycombinator.com, trustmrr.com, vibedna.ai, alternativeto.net and others were blocked by the egress proxy). Every finding below therefore comes from search-result extracts of the linked page, not a full read. Figures from TrustMRR, Indie Hackers, Blind and vendor blogs are self-reported unless stated; conflicts between snapshots are flagged in place. Revenue disclosures for the named Claude Code companions (Omnara, Happy, Pushary, Conductor, ccusage) simply do not exist in public as of this search; that absence is itself a key finding.

## Key Question 1: What is the realistic range of MRR a solo founder reached within 90 days of launch for a developer-facing tool in this space, with named examples?

### Takeaway
The verifiable range is wide and bimodal: most dev-tool side projects sit between $0 and a few hundred dollars MRR (median micro-SaaS ~$145 to $500/month across large self-reported datasets), while a handful of named outliers (21st.dev Magic MCP, ClaudeKit, AI Toolbox) reached roughly $4k to $17k/month, and only one of those (21st.dev, unverified) did it inside 90 days. None of the best-known Claude Code companions (Omnara, Happy, Pushary, Conductor, Vibe Kanban, ccusage) has disclosed MRR; the one with the most users (Vibe Kanban, ~28k stars, "thousands" daily users) shut down for lack of a business model.

### Cited Findings

Named examples in or adjacent to the Claude Code / MCP space:
- 21st.dev (Magic MCP, UI-component generation MCP server): a solo developer launched it in summer 2025 with zero marketing budget and reportedly hit $10,000 MRR in 6 weeks on tiers of $49, $299 and $999/month; the same article says fewer than 5% of 12,000+ public MCP servers earn money — [arte.itlibra.com "Can MCP servers be monetized"](https://arte.itlibra.com/en/articles/can-mcp-servers-be-monetized) (secondary; no primary founder statement found). The $10K-in-6-weeks claim with "5 free requests then paid" is repeated by [VibeDNA, State of MCP servers 2026](https://vibedna.ai/blog/state-of-mcp-servers-2026), which calls it "the headline case, not the median" and puts a realistic first-year target for a genuinely useful niche MCP server at $500 to $5,000/month.
- ClaudeKit (bundle of 122 slash commands, 26 skills, 16 subagents across 6 kits, with an npm CLI; founder Madni Aghadi): launched June 12, 2026; priced $14.99/mo (single kit), $29.99/mo (Pro, 3 kits), $49.99/mo (All-Access), or $99 one-time per kit (Lifetime); listed for sale on TrustMRR at $50,000. TrustMRR snapshots conflict: one shows $3.7k in 30-day revenue against a $60k ask, another $12k/30 days against $90k, and a different ClaudeKit listing shows ~$81K all-time revenue with only $20 MRR — [TrustMRR claudekit-1](https://trustmrr.com/startup/claudekit-1); [TrustMRR claudekit](https://trustmrr.com/startup/claudekit). Interpretation: revenue is overwhelmingly one-time/lifetime, not recurring.
- ClockedCode (Claude Code-related): $123 all-time verified revenue, no MRR — [TrustMRR clockedcode](https://trustmrr.com/startup/clockedcode).
- "Claude Code Distribution Framework": $235 all-time verified revenue, no current MRR — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/claude-code-distribution-framework).
- CodeBreak (macOS companion that alerts when Claude Code needs you): one-time $7; listed on TrustMRR with no revenue figure surfaced — [TrustMRR codebreak](https://trustmrr.com/startup/codebreak?metric=mrr).
- Omnara (YC S25; founders Ishaan Sehgal, Kartik Sarangmath, plus "Christian" on the HN post): Show HN in August 2025 said free for up to 10 agent sessions/month, then $9/month unlimited — [Show HN: Omnara](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44878650); YC profile [ycombinator.com/companies/omnara](https://ycombinator.com/companies/omnara). - Happy Coder (slopus/happy): free, MIT, ~21,233 GitHub stars per aggregator; App Store review counts conflict (9 vs 594); no monetization or revenue found — [sourcepulse](https://www.sourcepulse.org/projects/12376471); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/jo/app/happy-codex-claude-code-app/id6748571505?l=ar).

Population benchmarks (self-reported datasets):
- Across 3,787 micro-SaaS startups reporting revenue, median MRR is ~$145 and average ~$4,298; only 6.1% clear $10K MRR — [blog.mean.ceo Tiny Team SaaS Statistics 2026](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=11177).
- Alternative roundup: average ~$1,735 MRR at 64% margin, median profitable product ~$4.2K MRR, median product overall ~$500/month — [Flowjam](https://flowjam.com/blog/27-micro-saas-examples-that-actually-print-money-in-2025).
- "87% of developer SaaS side projects never reach $500 MRR" — [jakeinsight.com](https://jakeinsight.com/side-income/2026-03-11-monthly-recurring-revenue-from-a-dev-tool-side-pro/) (low-authority blog; methodology not shown).
- "Median active indie hackers make under $1K/month; top 10% make $10K+ MRR" — [betterlaunch.co](https://www.betterlaunch.co/blog/indie-hacker) (secondary).

### Inferences
- For a solo founder in this niche, "high confidence of being paid" realistically means $500 to $3,000/month within 90 days if the tool solves a sharp, repeated pain and is priced at $7 to $20 (or sells a lifetime licence); $10K MRR in 90 days has exactly one claimed example (21st.dev) and it sells a generated artefact (UI components) with $49+ tiers, not a workflow utility.
- One-time/lifetime pricing dominates actual cash in this niche (ClaudeKit ~$81K all-time vs $20 MRR; AI Toolbox ~75% lifetime). MRR figures for developer utilities are small even when total revenue is respectable.
- Every well-known free, open-source companion (Happy, Vibe Kanban, ccusage, Conductor) has either no revenue or shut down; being the most-starred tool in the category did not translate to payment.

### Gaps
- No public MRR or paying-customer counts exist for Omnara, Happy, Pushary, Conductor, ccusage or any Claude Code mobile companion; no Stripe screenshots or build-in-public threads with numbers surfaced for them.
- The 21st.dev $10K/6-week figure is only in secondary blogs; no founder tweet or primary post was retrievable.
- TrustMRR ClaudeKit snapshots conflict ($3.7k vs $12k 30-day; $20 MRR vs $5.7k-style figures) and could not be reconciled without fetching the live page.
- No cohort data on "developer tools launched in 2025, MRR at day 90" exists in any source found.

## Key Question 2: Which channel converted best for a $5 to $20 per month developer tool?

### Takeaway
The channels with evidence of producing paying users are (1) platform app directories where the buyer already lives (Slack/Teams App Directory for Karma bot, Chrome Web Store for AI Toolbox, MCP clients for 21st.dev) and (2) Reddit/Indie Hackers community posts for the first subscribers; Product Hunt and Show HN produce bursts of attention with fast decay and weak evidence of conversion. No source offered head-to-head conversion data for a $5 to $20/month dev tool specifically.

### Cited Findings
- Karma bot (Slack): ~$33K MRR, ARPU ~$100, and "most of its traffic comes from the Slack and MS Teams marketplaces" — [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/blog/karma-bot) (undated newsletter).
- Standuply (Slack standup bot, 2018 interview): charges $2 to $4 per user; being featured on the Slack App Directory main page in March 2017 drove a large signup spike — [Indie Hackers interview](https://www.indiehackers.com/interview/going-all-in-with-our-savings-to-build-a-business-on-a-new-platform-3501f0b9cd).
- Abot (anonymous Slack bot, solo founder): installed by 5k+ Slack teams, $4k MRR, "took over five years" — [pawelurbanek.com](https://pawelurbanek.com/anonymous-slack-bot-income).
- Counter-example: a solo founder's Slack bot had only 3 users one week after launch; commenter: "the Slack App Directory is not used very much" (no data) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-was-excited-to-launch-my-slack-bot-but-only-have-3-users-after-one-week-f3c5c01065).
- Chrome Web Store: AI Toolbox converted 40,000+ installs into 1,700+ paying customers (~4%), mostly lifetime plans — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/ai-toolbox).
- MCP client distribution: 21st.dev reportedly reached $10K MRR with "5 free requests then paid" and "zero marketing spend" — [VibeDNA](https://vibedna.ai/blog/state-of-mcp-servers-2026) (secondary).
- Reddit: SQL chatbot founder posted on Reddit, got first $19.99 subscriber two months later, and reached ~$1k MRR before ever launching on Product Hunt; argues PH is "a credibility signal (for press, investors, partnerships) rather than a customer acquisition channel" — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/why-i-still-havent-launched-on-product-hunt-at-1k-mrr-Y6F471gvNv7n6rPzznSj).
- Product Hunt: a mixed-experience first-launch retrospective — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/mixed-experience-with-our-first-producthunt-launch-Sumd0UNdezG4znGUzfRN); a vendor blog claims a 2024 study of 387 launches showed 23.1% conversion per engaged Indie Hackers post vs 3.1% per Product Hunt launch — [awesome-directories.com](https://awesome-directories.com/blog/indie-hackers-launch-strategy-guide-2025/) (vendor marketing; study not verifiable).
- Hacker News: Show HN traffic "peaks fast, with a half-life of about 24 hours"; rule of thumb 1.4 GitHub stars per HN upvote within 48 hours; claims of 500 to 2,000 signups in 24 hours are unverified — [daily.dev business blog](https://business.daily.dev/resources/hacker-news-marketing-developer-tools-show-hn-launch-day-sustained-coverage/).
- VS Code Marketplace: no paid extensions, no native subscriptions, no tip jars; the workaround is a free listing plus self-validated licence keys; one licensing service (code-checkout) takes a 10% fee — [Dodo Payments guide](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/sell-vscode-extensions); [dev.to](https://dev.to/shawnroller/vscode-extensions-adding-paid-features-1noa). Developer sentiment (2020 thread): skeptical of paying for editor plugins "unless work was paying" — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/have-you-paid-for-a-vscode-extension-4b84a5e762).
- GitHub Marketplace: no indie revenue case studies found; a fueler.io estimate of "$200M annually" for the whole Marketplace has no methodology — [fueler.io](https://fueler.io/blog/github-usage-revenue-valuation-growth-statistics) (unverified).
- Claude Code plugin marketplaces: distribution is any git repo/URL with `.claude-plugin/marketplace.json`; install via `/plugin marketplace add user-or-org/repo` — [Anthropic](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-code-plugins). A third-party guide says some publishers "charge a subscription for premium tiers or require a paid account on their platform" and that fees are listed on the plugin detail page — [systemprompt.io](https://systemprompt.io/guides/getting-started-anthropic-marketplace) (secondary).
- Ad-supported alternative channel inside Claude Code: WaitLine sells a sponsored line in the idle spinner and pays the tool author 50% of impressions; ADtention does the same in the statusline; claudemarketplaces.com sells 8 sponsor slots — [Product Hunt WaitLine](https://www.producthunt.com/products/waitline-2); [PeerPush ADtention](https://peerpush.com/p/adtention); [claudemarketplaces.com](https://claudemarketplaces.com/go/advertise-cta).
- Pricing-band advice (opinion): "sweet spot for indie developer tools is $9-$29/month for individuals and $29-$99/month for teams" — [revenuefast.in](https://revenuefast.in/grow/developer-tool-first-1000-users); counter-opinion "pricing below $29/mo is a trap: churn eats you alive" — [jakeinsight.com](https://jakeinsight.com/side-income/2026-03-11-monthly-recurring-revenue-from-a-dev-tool-side-pro/).

### Inferences
- For a $5 to $20/month dev tool, the strongest signal is "be installable from the surface where the agent already runs" (MCP client, Chrome Web Store, Slack/Teams directory, npm `npx ... setup` one-liners as Pushary does), with Reddit/Indie Hackers posts to seed the first dozen subscribers. Launch platforms are for credibility and backlinks.
- Marketplaces that handle billing (GitHub Marketplace, Slack directory via your own Stripe) remove friction; marketplaces with no billing (VS Code, Claude Code plugins, MCP registry) require licence-key or hosted-account gating, which is what every paid example in this niche does.

### Gaps
- No source gives conversion rates by channel for a developer tool at $5 to $20/month; the Karma bot and AI Toolbox figures are the closest proxies.
- Nothing found on GitHub Marketplace paid-app outcomes for indie publishers in 2025-2026.
- r/ClaudeAI self-promotion rules and launch outcomes were not retrievable.

## Key Question 3: What distinguished the tools that were paid from the ones that were only starred?

### Takeaway
Paid tools in this niche either (a) deliver something the agent cannot already do and that has an obvious cash value (generated UI components, a mobile approval loop, cross-tool privacy-preserving search), or (b) are sold as cheap one-time licences for a native app that feels like a product rather than a script. Starred-but-unpaid tools are open-source wrappers of capabilities the platform vendor is likely to ship (session mirroring, usage dashboards, kanban orchestration), with free tiers so generous that "thousands of daily users" produced no viable business.

### Cited Findings
- Vibe Kanban: 27.9k stars, Apache-2.0, "thousands of software engineers use it every day" but "the vast majority are free users"; shut down April 10, 2026 — [vibekanban.com/blog/shutdown](https://www.vibekanban.com/blog/shutdown); README now carries a sunsetting banner and a third-party site reports no commits for 117 days — [first-tree.ai](https://first-tree.ai/compare/vs-vibe-kanban) (secondary).
- Happy: ~21k stars, free, MIT, "not affiliated with Anthropic or OpenAI"; a reviewer "wanted to donate", implying no paid path — [appmatch.jp](https://appmatch.jp/?p=179967); [sourcepulse](https://www.sourcepulse.org/projects/12376471).
- ccusage: viral, free; monetization in that niche has instead gone to ad placement in idle time (WaitLine 50% rev-share) — [lilys.ai summary](https://lilys.ai/it/notes/claude-code-20260119/ccusage-code-cost-viral); [WaitLine](https://www.producthunt.com/products/waitline-2).
- Most public MCP servers are "wrappers around official APIs that the LLM can already call directly, or rebuilds of official servers from the vendor itself" — [VibeDNA](https://vibedna.ai/blog/state-of-mcp-servers-2026); "fewer than 5% of 12,000+ public MCP servers earn money" — [arte.itlibra.com](https://arte.itlibra.com/en/articles/can-mcp-servers-be-monetized) (unverified industry claim).
- Paid: Pushary's pitch is explicitly the two-way approval flow ("answer from your lock screen and Claude continues"), contrasted with the free one-way ntfy Stop-hook alternative — [pushary.com](https://pushary.com/claude-code-notifications). Omnara charged $9/month for unlimited sessions with one-tap approvals — [Show HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44878650); [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/omnara).
- Paid: AI Toolbox's differentiators are cross-vendor (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Grok) and "privacy-first, no chat content leaving the browser"; 75% of revenue from lifetime plans — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/ai-toolbox).
- Paid: ClaudeKit sells packaged prompts/skills at $14.99 to $49.99/month or $99 lifetime, $81K all-time — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/claudekit); a Gumroad "Claude CodePro" course/kit at €49/month — [Gumroad](https://maxritter.gumroad.com/l/claude-codepro).
- Paid: small native Mac/iOS apps at $0.99 to $69 one-time (Code Meter, SessionWatcher, CodeBreak, Claudy, ClaudeWatch) coexist with a free MIT "Claude Usage Tracker" — [sessionwatcher comparison](https://sessionwatcher.com/guides/sessionwatcher-vs-claude-usage-tracker).
- Pricing form in the open-source-with-revenue model: one single-developer open-source company makes $14.2k/month by charging "only for cloud costs, never force developers to pay" — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-did-it-my-open-source-company-now-makes-14-2k-monthly-as-a-single-developer-f2fec088a4).
- Official Claude Code docs warn plugins and marketplaces "can execute arbitrary code on a machine with user privileges", a trust barrier for paid third-party plugins — [rubinlake.com](https://rubinlake.com/en/technology-radar/developer-ai-and-delivery/claude-code-plugin-marketplace) (quoting the docs).

### Inferences
- The paid set shares three traits: a hosted or on-device component the user cannot trivially replicate (push relay, local index, component generator), a price under $10/month or a one-time licence under $100, and installation in under a minute from a surface the developer already uses (npx, Chrome Web Store, MCP config).
- The starred set shares: open-source mirror/dashboard functionality, no hosted dependency, and a value proposition that Anthropic later shipped natively (see Q4).
- Lifetime pricing converts developers who are skeptical of subscriptions for utilities; subscriptions work when the tool is part of daily production workflow with a team (Karma bot) or metered output (21st.dev).

### Gaps
- No controlled comparison of paid vs unpaid tools exists; the above is pattern-matching across a dozen cases.
- Could not verify Happy's or Conductor's internal business plans (e.g. whether Conductor Cloud will be paid).

## Key Question 4: How quickly did platform vendors absorb popular indie features, with dated examples?

### Takeaway
Anthropic shipped native versions of the most popular indie Claude Code add-ons within roughly 6 to 9 months of their rise: checkpoints (Sep 29, 2025), plugins/marketplaces (Oct 9, 2025), cloud sessions + iOS (Oct 20, 2025), Auto Memory (Feb 2026), Remote Control for phones (Feb 25, 2026), Channels for Telegram/Discord/iMessage (Mar 20, 2026), a parallel-session desktop app (Apr 14, 2026, four days after Vibe Kanban's shutdown), and Mods (Oct 2026). Third-party mobile companions survived by moving to multi-agent (Codex, Amp, Droid) support that Remote Control does not offer.

### Cited Findings
- Sep 29, 2025: Claude Code 2.0 adds checkpoints and `/rewind` (undo code, conversation or both), plus native VS Code extension — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/enabling-claude-code-to-work-more-autonomously); [claudelog](https://claudelog.com/faqs/how-to-use-checkpoints-in-claude-code/).
- Oct 9, 2025: Plugins public beta with marketplaces (any git repo/URL with `.claude-plugin/marketplace.json`) — [Anthropic](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-code-plugins).

### Inferences

- For a founder with Slack/Teams/phone delivery: Channels covers Telegram/Discord/iMessage but not Slack or Teams as of the docs found, and Remote Control is single-session and Pro/Max only; Team/Enterprise plans were excluded at Remote Control launch, which is where paying teams sit.

## Key Question 5 (objective part e): Rules and fees for GitHub Marketplace, Claude Code plugin marketplace, MCP registry/Anthropic directory, and Stripe/merchant-of-record options

### Takeaway
GitHub Marketplace is the only developer-tool marketplace in scope that bills for you (5% fee, $500 payout minimum, verified-publisher org and 100-install threshold); Claude Code plugin marketplaces, the official MCP registry and the VS Code Marketplace have no payment layer, so paid tiers must be gated by licence keys or hosted accounts. For global solo selling, every merchant-of-record option (Lemon Squeezy, Paddle, Polar for new orgs, Stripe Managed Payments) now costs roughly 5% + $0.50 or more per transaction before international surcharges, versus ~2.9% + $0.30 + 0.5% tax add-on on plain Stripe where you carry tax compliance yourself.

### Cited Findings
GitHub Marketplace:
- Transactions after Jan 1, 2021: GitHub retains 5% (25% before); payout arrives the month after cumulative revenue reaches $500 minimum — [GitHub docs, receiving payment](https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-cloud@latest/apps/github-marketplace/selling-your-app-on-github-marketplace/receiving-payment-for-app-purchases); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/developers-can-now-easily-sell-their-tools-on-github-marketplace).
- Paid plans require the app to be owned by an organization that is a verified publisher with financial onboarding; GitHub Apps need at least 100 installations (OAuth apps 200 users); apps must handle purchase and cancellation events; if a paid version is sold outside the Marketplace, a paid plan must also be offered inside once eligible — [GitHub docs, requirements](https://docs.github.com/en/apps/github-marketplace/creating-apps-for-github-marketplace/requirements-for-listing-an-app); [pricing plans](https://docs.github.com/en/apps/github-marketplace/selling-your-app-on-github-marketplace/pricing-plans-for-github-marketplace-apps). Up to ten plans across free, flat-rate and per-unit (third-party summary) — [Octopus](https://octopus.com/devops/github-actions/github-marketplace-price/).

Claude Code plugin marketplace:
- Launched Oct 9, 2025 (public beta); a marketplace is any git/GitHub repo or URL with `.claude-plugin/marketplace.json`; organizations can distribute approved plugins internally — [Anthropic](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-code-plugins); official docs on creating marketplaces — [code.claude.com/docs/en/plugin-marketplaces](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugin-marketplaces); Anthropic-run `claude-plugins-official` is curated, and the community marketplace lists plugins that passed automated validation and safety screening — [code.claude.com discover-plugins](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/discover-plugins?m=1); [GitHub anthropics/claude-plugins-official](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-plugins-official).
- No official paid-plugin, billing or licence-key mechanism appears in any doc or guide found; private marketplaces rely on git-host access control; publishers gate premium tiers behind their own subscriptions/accounts (third-party guide) — [systemprompt.io](https://systemprompt.io/guides/getting-started-anthropic-marketplace); a 2026 guide claims submitting to Anthropic's directory requires a paid claude.ai plan — [dev.to](https://dev.to/akaranjkar08/claude-code-plugin-marketplace-2026-package-and-publish-50nd) (single secondary source).

Anthropic connectors (MCP) directory and official MCP registry:
- Submission requirements: security standards, tool annotations, OAuth 2.0 for authenticated services, documentation, privacy policy for local connectors; a test account if login is required; remote servers submitted via the claude.ai admin portal, "always open" — [claude.com/docs/connectors/building/submission](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/submission); [directory docs](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/directory).
- Plan requirement conflicts: official FAQ says "any organization can submit regardless of plan type" — [support.claude.com FAQ](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11596036-anthropic-mcp-directory-faq); a news report says "anyone on a paid Claude plan" — [pasqualepillitteri.it](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/18444/anthropic-portal-submit-plugins-mcp-connectors-claude); a vendor guide says Team/Enterprise org required — [manufact.com](https://manufact.com/blog/submit-mcp-server-to-anthropic-directory).
- Official MCP registry: names are reverse-DNS namespaces tied to a verified GitHub account or domain; metadata hosting only, no code hosting or security certification; still a preview service; listing "proves neither security nor production quality" — [mcpplaygroundonline guide](https://mcpplaygroundonline.com/blog/how-to-publish-your-mcp-server-to-registry.md); [dev.to registry by the numbers](https://dev.to/amareswer/the-mcp-registry-by-the-numbers-38nc). No rule on paid servers found; a separate commercial directory (mctx "MCP Community Registry") requires payout setup — [docs.mctx.ai](https://docs.mctx.ai/distribute/mcp-registry).

VS Code Marketplace: no paid extensions, subscriptions or tip jars; "pricing" field offers only Free / Free Trial — [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/sell-vscode-extensions); [dev.to](https://dev.to/shawnroller/vscode-extensions-adding-paid-features-1noa).

Payments / merchant of record (all vendor or competitor pages; verify live pricing):
- Plain Stripe: ~2.9% + $0.30 + 0.5% Stripe Tax; not a merchant of record, seller liable for global tax — [rework.com comparison](https://resources.rework.com/tools/billing-revenue/paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy).
- Stripe Managed Payments (MoR): public preview Feb 2026, full MoR by April 2026; +3.5% on top of standard fees (~6.4% + $0.30 domestic; 8 to 10% all-in international per competitor); works with Checkout and Payment Links, not Elements; subscriptions require Stripe Billing; not available via RevenueCat Billing — [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/stripe-managed-payments-fees-explained); [UserJot](https://userjot.com/blog/stripe-managed-payments-for-saas); [RevenueCat docs](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/web/integrations/stripe/stripe-managed-payments.md); [fungies.io timeline](https://fungies.io/lemon-squeezy-stripe-acquisition-saas-founders-2026/).
- Lemon Squeezy: 5% + $0.50, plus +1.5% international cards, +1.5% PayPal, +0.5% subscriptions on some plans; acquired by Stripe July 2024; still operating standalone as of April 2026 with a migration path to Managed Payments — [Dodo review](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/lemonsqueezy-review); [fungies.io](https://fungies.io/?p=39398); [Finextra](https://finextra.com/pressarticle/101686/stripe-acquires-lemon-squeezy).
- Paddle: 5% + $0.50, no monthly fee; "closest replacement for Lemon Squeezy" — [Dodo comparison](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy/).
- Polar.sh: for organizations created on or after May 27, 2026 the free tier is 5% + $0.50 (previously 4% + $0.40); optional Pro/Growth/Scale plans (third-party: $20/$100/$400 per month); international card, payout and dispute fees extra (competitor: +1.5% intl, +0.5% subscriptions, $15 chargeback) — [Polar blog, Introducing Polar Plans](https://polar.sh/blog/introducing-polar-plans); [fungies.io review](https://fungies.io/polar-sh-review-2026); [Dodo review](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/polar-sh-review).
- Licence-key services for IDE extensions: code-checkout takes 10% — [dev.to](https://dev.to/shawnroller/vscode-extensions-adding-paid-features-1noa).

### Inferences
- For a solo founder with an existing API/SDK, the cheapest compliant global path is a MoR at ~5% + $0.50 (Paddle or Lemon Squeezy; Polar if already on the legacy 4% plan) attached to licence keys or hosted-account gating, since none of the agent-native marketplaces (Claude Code plugins, MCP registry, VS Code) bill. GitHub Marketplace is worth a listing only after 100 installs and an org-level verified publisher, at which point its 5% fee is competitive with any MoR.
- Expect to lose roughly 6 to 8% of gross on international developer purchases under any MoR once surcharges apply; at $9/month that is ~$0.95 per subscription per month.

- Official Claude Code plugin docs were not fetchable to confirm the absence of a billing field; absence is inferred from all guides found.
- Whether the official MCP registry prohibits, allows or ignores paid servers is undocumented in any retrieved source.
