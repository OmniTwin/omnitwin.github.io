# Do developers and small teams pay for organizational context for AI coding agents? (as of 16 Oct 2026)

Scope: the paid "context engine" category, and the specific friction of decisions made in Slack, Teams, meetings and PRs that coding agents never see. This deliberately does not repeat the earlier inventory in `../Monetizable slice for Decidio/team_context_tools.md` (Context7, Supermemory, Mem0, Letta, claude-mem, Ruler, ContextStream, Tandem, Hjarni, Windsurf memories, Cursor/Anthropic/Copilot rule distribution).

Method note: 55 web searches. Every vendor site I tried was blocked from this environment (getunblocked.com, augmentcode.com, survey.stackoverflow.co, docs.slack.dev and docs.glean.com all failed DNS or were refused by the proxy), so **nearly every vendor fact below comes from search-engine summaries of the linked page, not from reading the page itself**. Third-party pricing trackers (costbench, usagepricing, pricingsaas, toolradar) often disagree with each other, and where they do I say so. Two kinds of source were read directly: the GitHub Copilot Spaces docs (raw GitHub docs source) and anthropics/claude-code issues (GitHub MCP search, with reaction counts as of 16 Oct 2026). GitHub's REST search endpoint was blocked. Reddit and Hacker News could not be searched directly and no relevant threads came back from web search. "(snippet)" marks a fact taken only from a search summary. "(vendor)" marks a vendor making claims about itself.

## Q1. Which products sell team or organizational context to coding agents, at what price, and with what traction?

### Takeaway
Paid context for coding agents now sells in two bands. **Self-serve tools cost $19 to $40 per seat per month**: Unblocked $29, Greptile $30, CodeRabbit $24, Qodo Teams $30, Graphite $40, Dust $24 to $30, Cursor Teams $40. **Enterprise context engines are sales-led**: Glean at about $50 to $75 per seat with roughly a 100-seat minimum, Sourcegraph from about $16K a year, Tabnine's and Qodo's context engines on custom quotes. Only one vendor explicitly sells "the why" from Slack, Teams, Jira and PRs to any MCP agent at a self-serve price: **Unblocked** ($29/user/mo annual, 21-day trial, no seat minimum, $20M Series A in May 2025, named customers Drata, TravelPerk and Clio). Even Unblocked infers decisions at query time from recency and authorship. It does not keep curated, sealed decision records. Most other "context engines" are about code (Augment, Sourcegraph, Greptile, Tabnine, Qodo). GitHub Copilot Spaces gives away curated, shared context for free. I found no seat or revenue numbers for any of the decision-specific newcomers (Settled, Decision Log, KodHau, adrkit, TrailMark).

### Inferences
- The category pays at $20 to $40 per seat when the product is attached to a workflow with a visible output, usually code review comments. Unblocked's entry tier is a code-review product, and its headline testimonial is about code review. "Context" on its own is sold as the reason the review is better, not as the thing being bought.
- Unblocked is the real incumbent for "Slack/PR decisions into coding agents" at small-team prices. Its own blog concedes the gap Decidio targets: search cannot tell which thread holds the current decision. It answers that with ranking heuristics, not with a human-sealed record.
- At least five near-copies of Decidio's register-plus-MCP idea appeared in 2026 (Settled, Decision Log, KodHau, adrkit, AdrMcp/Lema), and none shows paying users. That points to an idea that is cheap to build and does not yet have proven willingness to pay.
- Copilot Spaces makes "curated, team-shared context packs readable by IDE agents" free for GitHub shops. That undercuts the "context pack" part of Decidio, but not Slack capture or the decision lifecycle.

### Gaps
- No ARR or customer counts for Unblocked, Augment Context Engine MCP, Dosu, Swimm or Pieces Teams. Unblocked's pricing page and docs could not be fetched, so the $19/$29 split is unconfirmed.
- I could not confirm Greptile's or CodeRabbit's "learnings" features or their Slack/Jira context from primary docs. Windsurf was covered in the earlier notes.
- Glean's MCP for coding agents and Notion's official MCP were not confirmed from primary sources. Unblocked's "Notion MCP" blog implies Notion MCP exists — [Unblocked Notion MCP](https://getunblocked.com/blog/notion-mcp/).

## Q2. Is there evidence developers pay specifically because agents lack "the why"?

### Takeaway
There is solid survey evidence that **missing context is a top-tier complaint**, though not the top one. In Qodo's 2025 survey, 65% said AI "misses relevant context". In 2026, 43% of engineering leaders called codebase context a major gap. Atlassian found 50% of developers lose 10+ hours a week to organizational inefficiencies, with finding information the biggest drain. In JetBrains' 2025 survey, by contrast, "lack of context awareness" ranks only fifth, at 10%. Stack Overflow's 2026 survey says colleagues (72%) are still the most trusted source of answers. I found **no direct evidence of anyone paying because an agent lacked a Slack or meeting decision**. The "agent reintroduced what we removed" story comes only from vendor demos (KodHau, Unblocked). The GitHub issue demand that does exist is about **one person's context following them across Claude surfaces** (#2511: 421 👍), not about team decisions captured from Slack.

### Cited findings

**Surveys**
- Qodo, 2025 State of AI Code Quality (609 developers): 65% of developers using AI for refactoring, and about 60% of those using it for testing, writing or review, say it "misses relevant context". 44% of those who think AI degrades quality blame missing context. "Better contextual understanding" was the top requested fix (26% of votes). — [Qodo report](https://www.qodo.ai/reports/state-of-ai-Code-quality/), [press release](https://aijourn.com/despite-78-claiming-productivity-gains-two-in-three-developers-say-ai-misses-critical-context-according-to-qodo-survey/) (snippet)

(snippet) — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/10/21/massive_jetbrains_dev_survey), [JetBrains Research](https://blog.jetbrains.com/research/2025/10/state-of-developer-ecosystem-2025/)

### Inferences
- The demand is real but generic. People feel the context gap mostly as code-level misunderstanding and as personal continuity across tools. Team decisions made in chat are a narrower subset, and the only people articulating it loudly are vendors.
- The strongest organic issue signal (421 + 161 + 124 + 96 👍) is for one person's context following them across Claude surfaces, which Anthropic will most likely ship natively. Decidio's "same pack in Claude Code and in Claude.ai/ChatGPT via MCP" answers it today, but it is a platform-gap play with a short shelf life.
- Since colleagues are still the most-trusted source (72%) and senior devs spend half their day on DMs, the economic pain is "senior engineers answering the same why-questions". That pain belongs to the engineering manager, which is a better buyer framing than "agents need context".

### Gaps
- No case study found where a team bought a tool after an agent reintroduced a removed pattern.
- The Stack Overflow 2026 context-question wording and percentages are unverified (see above). GitHub Octoverse 2025/2026 was not checked; I ran out of search budget.
- No Cursor-repo or Cursor-forum data. forum.cursor.com could not be searched directly.

## Q3. Who captures decisions from Slack and meetings into a durable, reviewable record, and does any of them push those decisions into coding agents?

### Takeaway
Meeting tools now extract "decisions and action items" as summary text, and most expose it over MCP: Granola, Fellow, Otter, Read AI (beta), and Notion through its own agents. Seat prices run **$7 to $20 per user per month**. None of them keeps decisions as typed, versioned objects with supersession, and none targets coding agents specifically. Slack has made a strategic move: **its own MCP server and Real-Time Search API went GA on 17 Feb 2026**, and **Slack Code (24 Aug 2026)** brings Claude Code, Devin, Copilot and ChatGPT agents into Slack channels on any Slack plan. Meanwhile **Slack's 2025 API terms ban bulk export, persistent indexes and use of API data in LLMs**, which pulled Slack out of Glean's knowledge graph. Raw Slack access for agents is now a free platform utility. A durable, reviewed decision record is not something the platform provides, and building one is now policy-constrained. The only products that capture decisions with review and lifecycle and also serve agents are tiny 2026 launches: Settled, Decision Log and Workspace Historian.

### Cited findings

**Meeting tools**
- Granola: MCP server at `mcp.granola.ai/mcp` (Feb 2026) for Claude Code, Cursor and ChatGPT. Business $14/user/mo for full history and transcripts; the free tier is limited to the last 30 days with no transcripts. Decisions are not exposed as a separate field. (snippet, affiliate blog) — [Market Correct: Granola MCP](https://mrktcorrect.com/blog/granola-mcp)
- Fellow: captures "decisions, action items, and key moments", attached to a shared agenda. Its MCP server lets Claude and ChatGPT read notes, recaps and action items (Fellow 5.0, 3 Dec 2025). Team $7, Business $15, Enterprise $25/user/mo annual (checked 3 Oct 2026). The free tier is 5 AI notes per user, lifetime. (snippet) — [Fellow MCP blog](https://fellow.ai/blog/fellow-mcp-server/), [Anavem](https://www.anavem.com/tools/fellow)
- Fireflies: Free, Pro $10, Business $19, Enterprise $39/user/mo annual; summaries and action items are gated by AI credits. MCP not confirmed in my searches. (snippet, competitor source) — [Sonix](https://sonix.ai/resources/fireflies-ai-pricing/)

## Q4. Is there a paid, self-serve product for a 3–30 person team that turns Slack/Teams/meeting decisions into curated, versioned, permission-aware context that Claude Code, Cursor and ChatGPT all read?

### Takeaway
**Not as a proven paid product.** **Unblocked** is the closest that is actually paid and self-serve: $29/user/mo, a trial, no seat minimum, Slack, Teams, PRs, Jira and Confluence, MCP to Claude Code, Cursor and Copilot, and permission enforcement. But it is retrieval with ranking heuristics, not a curated or versioned register. Agent access from ChatGPT is not confirmed, and $290/mo for a 10-person team is a real line item. **Decision Log** and **Settled** match the curated, superseded, agent-readable model almost exactly, but have no visible price or traction. **Copilot Spaces** matches "curated and shared" for free but ignores Slack. The complaints that come up are vendor-reported staleness ("prose has no compiler"; an eight-month-old Confluence page), "which thread is the current decision", and the security exposure of letting agents read Slack. A zero-click exfiltration CVE hit Anthropic's own Slack MCP server, which was archived rather than patched.

### Cited findings
- Unblocked: price, sources, MCP and permissions as in Q1. "Private channels... respect membership" with "an approval step" before sharing to public. (snippet, vendor) — [Unblocked Slack integration](https://getunblocked.com/integrations/slack/)
- Unblocked also admits that search cannot pick the current decision (Q1) — [Unblocked Slack MCP blog](https://getunblocked.com/blog/slack-mcp/)
- Staleness as a known failure: "prose has no compiler. Nothing fails when a rule goes stale" — [Unblocked: rules file rot](https://getunblocked.com/blog/rules-file-rot) (from earlier notes); "vector indexes keep serving superseded facts" (snippet, vendor) — [Unblocked](https://getunblocked.com/blog/building-ai-code-review-with-context-as-a-first-class-system)
- Decision Log: append-only, supersession links, review queue for agent suggestions, MCP. Settled: proposed → contested → settled → superseded, with an agent "binding?" check. Prices not found. (snippet) — [Decision Log](https://mcpservers.org/servers/www-decisionlog-ai), [Settled](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/GHGuide/settled)
- Copilot Spaces: free on all Copilot tiers, org-shared roles, auto-synced GitHub sources, accepts transcripts and notes as free text, readable over the GitHub MCP server. (primary) — [github/docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/docs/main/content/copilot/concepts/context/spaces.md)
- Slack data privacy:
  - CVE-2025-34072: in Anthropic's (now archived) Slack MCP server, prompt injection made an agent embed private data in links that Slack's link-preview bots then fetched, giving zero-click exfiltration. Reported as archived and not patched. (snippet) — [Debian tracker](https://security-tracker.debian.org/tracker/CVE-2025-34072), [Strac](https://www.strac.io/blog/slack-mcp-server)
  - "Any Slack MCP server... returns whatever the connected account can see" (snippet) — [Strac](https://www.strac.io/blog/slack-mcp-server)
  - 2024 Slack AI private-channel leak via a poisoned public channel (patched) — [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Aug/20/data-exfiltration-from-slack-ai/)
- Glean lost Slack in its knowledge graph under the new terms (Q3) — [Glean docs](https://docs.glean.com/connectors/native/slack/about/slack-connector-changes)

### Inferences
- The gap is open in form (curated, sealed, superseded) and contested in function: Unblocked plus the free Slack MCP give agents "an answer about why" that is good enough for many teams.
- The curation step is both Decidio's moat and its main friction. Someone has to review and seal, and ADR practice shows most teams will not do it unless the tool turns sealing into a byproduct of work they already do (a reaction, a PR merge, a meeting close).
- Decidio's tokenization (role tokens instead of names and vendor data) plus storing only sealed derived records is a real differentiator against index-everything tools, given Slack's terms and the exfiltration CVEs. I found no evidence, though, that 3–30 person teams choose tools for this reason. It is more likely to matter in regulated or agency settings.

### Gaps
- No independent reviews (G2, Reddit, HN) of Unblocked, Decision Log or Settled were found, so customer complaints are vendor-stated.
- Whether Unblocked's MCP works with ChatGPT developer mode or claude.ai connectors is unconfirmed.

## Q5. Willingness-to-pay signals: price points, conversion, buyer

### Takeaway
For team context and code-review tools, the price that self-serve buyers clearly pay is **$20 to $40 per seat per month**. Unblocked ($29), Greptile ($30), CodeRabbit ($24), Qodo ($30), Graphite ($40) and Cursor Teams ($40) all sit there. Meeting and notes tools cluster at **$7 to $20**. **Flat per-workspace pricing is appearing**: Augment Business is $100/mo for up to 50 seats, and the earlier notes found Supermemory with unlimited users. Engineering leaders now budget **$500 to $1,000 per developer per year** as the floor for AI tooling. That leaves room for a $10 to $25/dev/mo add-on, but it competes with metered agent spend that finance is starting to scrutinize. I found no conversion stories and no evidence about card-wielding engineering managers specifically. Unblocked's trial with no seat minimum suggests the category believes in self-serve entry and sales-assisted expansion.

## Ratings

- **Demand evidence: moderate in general, weak for paid decision memory.** Missing context is a top-five complaint (Qodo 65%; 43% of leaders; Atlassian 10+ hours a week), but the "agent ignored a Slack decision" pain is vendor-articulated, with no organic threads or purchase stories found.
- **Competitive gap: contested.** Unblocked ($29/seat) owns "why from Slack/PRs to agents". Slack's free MCP/RTS, Slack Code, ChatGPT company knowledge and free Copilot Spaces commoditize access. Five or more tiny decision-ledger MCPs (Settled, Decision Log, KodHau, adrkit, AdrMcp) chase the curated model with no traction. The only open sliver is human-sealed, superseded, cross-vendor records.
- **Self-serve buyability: moderate.** A $20–40/seat card purchase is normal, and Unblocked shows trial-without-minimum works. But curation needs behavior change, and seat budgets are being squeezed by metered agent spend.

## Sharpest wedge for Decidio's existing assets

**"Decision conflict check" on PRs and agent plans, priced per workspace.** This is a GitHub App, Claude Code hook or MCP tool that, when a PR or an agent's plan touches an area covered by a sealed decision, posts the in-effect decision (owner, date, rationale, Slack/Teams permalink). It also flags when a change revives something a decision **superseded or retired**. Why this one:
1. It sells into the budget that already pays ($20–40/seat code review), where context vendors actually monetize, instead of selling "context" as such.
2. It uses the asset rivals lack. Unblocked admits search cannot tell which thread holds the current decision, and Decidio's register answers "in effect / replaced / retired" exactly, with an owner.
3. The output is measurable ("caught N reversals of sealed decisions"), which is the evidence a buyer needs and the thing missing everywhere in this research.
4. Capture from Slack and Teams becomes the feeder that keeps the register fresh, not the product. Tokenization and storing only sealed records are a compliance story that fits Slack's 2025 terms better than raw indexing.
5. Pricing to test: free for one repo and 25 sealed decisions, then about $49–$99/workspace/mo for 3–30 people. That undercuts Unblocked at 3+ seats.

If that check does not convert in a few design-partner teams, the honest conclusion is that **there is no standalone paid wedge in "organizable context" for small teams**. The idea is being built repeatedly by others for free, and platforms are absorbing access. Decidio's context packs would then be a retention feature of its decision register and approvals product, not a revenue line.
