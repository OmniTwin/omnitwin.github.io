# Sell the approval gate vendors refuse to build

No candidate slice reaches the "high confidence" bar the question asks for, because across all six research notes nobody disclosed revenue for a self-serve agent-approval, redaction or context product; the closest the evidence gets is Pushary selling phone approvals for coding agents at $9.99–$19.99/month with a #2 Product Hunt day (387 votes) and no published numbers. Within that constraint the single best bet is a **receipted, blocking approval gate for coding-agent actions that the operator is not watching**: scheduled or cloud Claude Code/Codex runs, and MCP/database writes that the free Bash guards never see, routed to Slack, Teams or phone, with the signed receipt as the thing no free tool offers. It ranks first because it is the only slice where all three demand signals coincide (press-level incidents, upvoted GitHub issues the vendors closed as "not planned", and an existing $9.99 price point), where the competitive gap is open rather than contested, and where Decidio's existing hook, gate API, routing and MCP annotations cover most of the build. The honest evidence grade is "strong demand, open gap, unproven willingness to pay", and one technical unknown (whether an HTTP hook fires inside a cloud session) must be tested on day one. Build an `npx` one-line installer with a default destructive-operations policy, price it at $9/month with a lifetime option, sell through a merchant-of-record rather than any marketplace, and judge it by paying installs within two weeks. The GitHub deploy gate for Team-plan private repos is the credible second bet; phone-approval-of-your-own-prompt, Bash command blocking, PII redaction for professionals, and context packs should be avoided for the reasons set out below.

## No slice shows people paying a solo founder, so ranking rests on asking and incidents

The question asks for a slice with demonstrated pull, so the ranking below weighs three demand signals (people asking, people paying, incidents) against competitive gap and against how much of Decidio's existing stack each slice reuses. The uncomfortable headline from the benchmarks note is that **none of the best-known Claude Code companions (Omnara, Happy, Pushary, Conductor, Vibe Kanban, ccusage) has ever disclosed MRR or paying-customer counts**, and the one with the most users, Vibe Kanban at roughly 28k stars and "thousands" of daily users, shut down in April 2026 because "the vast majority are free users and we couldn't find a business model" ([Vibe Kanban shutdown](https://www.vibekanban.com/blog/shutdown)). Across 3,787 micro-SaaS startups reporting revenue the median MRR is about **$145** and only 6.1% clear $10k ([Tiny Team SaaS Statistics 2026](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=11177)). A realistic 90-day outcome for a sharp developer utility at $7–20 is therefore $500–$3,000/month, and the one claimed counter-example (21st.dev's $10k MRR in six weeks) exists only in secondary blogs with no founder statement ([VibeDNA](https://vibedna.ai/blog/state-of-mcp-servers-2026)).

Two patterns in this table decide the recommendation. First, **the only places people demonstrably pay are places vendors have since closed**: Pushary and Forge Remote sell phone approval at $9.99/month ([toolradar Pushary pricing](https://toolradar.com/tools/pushary/pricing); [Forge Remote](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6760141378)), but Anthropic's Remote Control now ships approve/deny from the phone on Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise plans at no extra cost ([Remote Control docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/remote-control)), and OpenAI closed Codex remote control as "available in preview" in May 2026 ([openai/codex #9224](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/9224)). Second, **the open gaps are open because vendors are moving the other way**: Claude Code's routines run shell commands and connector writes "all without stopping for approval" ([Routines docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/routines)), auto mode became the default starting mode in v2.1.283 ([Permission modes](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permission-modes)), and the request to "send permission requests to Slack, email, or a webhook, and resume the agent once the user responds" was closed as not planned without a maintainer reply ([anthropics/claude-code #44771](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/44771)). A product that routes an approval to a person who is not at the terminal is therefore an orphan use case at both vendors, which is exactly the position a solo founder wants.

The notes disagree on how to grade two of these rows, and the table reflects my resolution. The personal-agent note rates OpenClaw outbound gating as "strong" demand, but its own evidence is four GitHub issues with 0–3 reactions whose closure reasons could not be read because the repository was not reachable, plus a 3.2M-monthly-active-user figure from third-party trackers ([gradually.ai](https://www.gradually.ai/en/openclaw-statistics/)); I downgrade that to moderate. The coding-agent note rates second-person approval "weak-to-moderate" while the personal-agent note rates it "moderate" for small businesses; both agree the gap is open and both found no direct feature request, so it belongs as an upsell inside slice 1, not a headline.

## Incidents and closed issues converge on unattended runs and MCP writes

The incidents that reached the press share one shape: no human was in the loop, and the damage came through a path that Bash-pattern guards do not inspect. In #13325, Opus in `acceptEdits` mode ran a Supabase MCP `UPDATE ... SET topic_standardized = NULL` that wiped about **55,000 rows** "without asking"; the reporter asked for guards that "Require an explicit 'yes' confirmation that shows the row count" and are "impossible to override through model reasoning", and the issue was closed "not planned" by a bot ([anthropics/claude-code #13325](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/13325)). In #21227 a `DROP TABLE` on production lost 1,508 records, of which 118 were recovered from agent logs, after which Claude spawned five background agents that burned the remaining API credits ([#21227](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/21227)). The DataTalks.Club `terraform destroy` with auto-approve on a stale state file deleted a database, VPC, ECS and "all automated backups" ([vibegraveyard](https://vibegraveyard.ai/story/claude-code-terraform-datatalks-infrastructure-destruction/)), and the "48,000 files in 103 seconds" sub-agent deletion is reported but unverified and reachable only through search summaries ([TechRadar](https://techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/do-not-let-programs-run-commands-on-your-machine-claude-code-allegedly-deleted-48-000-files-in-103-seconds-and-its-a-terrifying-warning-about-ai-agents)). The two database incidents went through MCP tools, not the shell, and **no widely adopted MCP-call guard surfaced** in the research, whereas the Bash guards dcg (6.2k stars) and cc-safety-net (1.6k stars) have soaked up all the free demand for command blocking ([dcg](https://github.com/Dicklesworthstone/destructive_command_guard); [cc-safety-net](https://github.com/kenryu42/cc-safety-net)).

The upvoted issues ask for precisely what Decidio's receipt already does. Codex #28969, open since June 2026 with a commenter citing about **197 thumbs-up**, complains that questions auto-resolve after 60 seconds so users "come back to decisions made for them" and that "a timeout should not count as human consent" ([openai/codex #28969](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/28969)); Anthropic had the same complaint at #73125 ("EXTREME DANGER") and made the timeout opt-in in July 2026 ([#73125](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/73125)). Codex #28437 asks for approvals "binding to exact input digests and returning audit receipts to the hook" so that "an external deterministic policy engine" can act as the approval layer ([openai/codex #28437](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/28437)), which is a specification of a signed, input-bound receipt. On the unattended side, #61015 ("Scheduled routines fail every MCP call with 'MCP tool call requires approval'", 43 in the search listing) and #47180 ("Cowork scheduled tasks ignore 'Always allow'", 39, open) show that scheduled runs currently either stall or run ungated ([issue search](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues?q=is%3Aissue+%22permission%22+%28slack+OR+telegram+OR+webhook+OR+mobile%29+approve+sort%3Areactions-%2B1-desc)), and Codex #23200 asks for approvals on always-on Linux hosts with 23 comments and no maintainer reply ([openai/codex #23200](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/23200)). Note that these listing counts are unlabelled and may be comments rather than reactions; GitHub hid reaction counts during the research.

Three things keep the evidence grade below "high". There is **no revenue or subscriber number for any approval product**, and the coding-agent note infers from a year of launches without a single MRR claim that revenue is small. The hook surface is leaky: PreToolUse hooks were not enforced on subagent calls until May 2026 (#21460), PermissionRequest hooks still do not fire for Agent Teams subagents (#23983), Codex hooks silently stopped running after a desktop update (#21639), and a timed-out hook "does not block the call" unless `onFailure: "block"` is set ([Hooks docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks)). And the central technical assumption, that a cloud session or routine will execute an `http` hook from a repository's committed `.claude/settings.json` and wait on it, is an inference the coding-agent note explicitly flags as untested: the docs confirm cloud sessions read repo settings and support `http` hooks, but say nothing about approval relays, and routines have no permission-mode picker at all ([Cloud docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-code-on-the-web); [Routines docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/routines)). If that test fails, the slice still works for locally scheduled runs (cron plus `claude -p`), which is where #47180 and #77817 live, and for the MCP-write gate, which runs wherever the hook runs.

Why this beats the deploy gate as the first move, despite the deploy gate's clean cost-avoidance story: GitHub's required reviewers, wait timers and custom protection rules are public-repo-only on Free, Pro and Team, so a 10-person team pays **$40 → $210/month** to get a deploy gate on a private repo ([GitHub docs source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/docs/main/content/actions/reference/workflows-and-actions/deployments-and-environments.md); [GitHub pricing](https://github.com/pricing)), and the de-facto workaround, trstringer/manual-approval with 677 stars, burns a billed runner for up to six hours and caps team approvers at 60 minutes because app tokens expire ([manual-approval](https://github.com/trstringer/manual-approval)). That is a real gap with a vacant price band between a free action and Sleuth's $36.3k median annual spend ([Vendr snippet](https://vendr.com/marketplace/sleuth-io)). But every community thread on it draws 0–2 votes ([discussion #202852](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/202852)), the two adjacent Marketplace apps have **3 and 4 installs** ([Greenlight](https://github.com/marketplace/greenlight-actions); [ApprovalOps](https://github.com/marketplace/approvalops)), a paid Marketplace listing needs a verified-publisher organization and about 100 installs first ([requirements](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/docs/main/content/apps/github-marketplace/creating-apps-for-github-marketplace/requirements-for-listing-an-app.md)), and the integration cannot use GitHub's native pending-deployment hook on Team private repos, so it needs a new GitHub App that ends the job and re-dispatches the deploy workflow. That is a slower path to the first dollar and the pricing comparables (Sleuth, Spacelift, Hoop.dev) are all aggregator snippets, none from a vendor page. It is the right second slice, and the two-week plan below probes it for free.

## Price at $9 with a lifetime option and install from the surface the agent already runs on

The benchmarks note is unambiguous about what separates paid from merely starred tools in this niche: paid tools have **a hosted component the user cannot trivially replicate, a price under $10/month or a one-time licence under $100, and installation in under a minute from a surface the developer already uses**, while the starred-and-unpaid tools are open-source mirrors of features the vendor later shipped. Pushary's pitch is explicitly the two-way loop ("answer from your lock screen and Claude continues") against the free one-way ntfy hook ([pushary.com](https://pushary.com/claude-code-notifications)), and AI Toolbox converted 40,000 Chrome installs into 1,700 paying customers with about 75% of revenue from lifetime plans ([TrustMRR ai-toolbox](https://trustmrr.com/startup/ai-toolbox)). ClaudeKit's TrustMRR snapshots conflict with each other ($3.7k versus $12k in 30-day revenue) but agree on the shape: roughly **$81k all-time against $20 MRR**, meaning developers bought lifetime licences and declined subscriptions ([TrustMRR claudekit](https://trustmrr.com/startup/claudekit)). For a product whose aim is to prove pull rather than maximize ARR, that argues for $9/month with a $49–79 lifetime tier and a free tier capped by approvals per month, not by features.

Distribution should bypass every agent-native marketplace, because none of them bills. The Claude Code plugin marketplace is any git repo with a `.claude-plugin/marketplace.json` and has no payment layer ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-code-plugins)); the official MCP registry hosts metadata only ([registry guide](https://mcpplaygroundonline.com/blog/how-to-publish-your-mcp-server-to-registry.md)); the VS Code Marketplace forbids paid extensions ([Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/sell-vscode-extensions)). The workable path is a free plugin or `npx` installer gated by a hosted account, with billing through a merchant of record at roughly **5% + $0.50 per transaction** (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, or Polar's post-May-2026 tier) so that global sales tax never becomes the founder's problem ([Paddle vs Lemon Squeezy](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy/); [Polar plans](https://polar.sh/blog/introducing-polar-plans)); all those fee figures are from vendor and competitor blogs, not live pricing pages. First subscribers in this niche have come from Reddit and Indie Hackers posts, not launch platforms: the SQL-chatbot founder got a first $19.99 subscriber two months after a Reddit post and treats Product Hunt as "a credibility signal rather than a customer acquisition channel" ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/why-i-still-havent-launched-on-product-hunt-at-1k-mrr-Y6F471gvNv7n6rPzznSj)), and Show HN traffic has "a half-life of about 24 hours" ([daily.dev](https://business.daily.dev/resources/hacker-news-marketing-developer-tools-show-hn-launch-day-sustained-coverage/)). GitHub Marketplace becomes worthwhile only after the deploy-gate slice reaches 100 installs, at which point its 5% fee is competitive ([developer agreement](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/docs/main/content/site-policy/github-terms/github-marketplace-developer-agreement.md)).

Budget the window honestly. Anthropic absorbed each popular single-platform companion feature within roughly six to nine months: checkpoints (Sep 2025), plugins (Oct 2025), cloud sessions and iOS (Oct 2025), Auto Memory (Feb 2026), Remote Control (Feb 2026, about six and a half months after Omnara's Show HN), Channels (Mar 2026), and a parallel-session desktop app four days after Vibe Kanban shut down ([VentureBeat on Remote Control](https://venturebeat.com/ai/anthropic-just-released-a-mobile-version-of-claude-code-called-remote); [Channels docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/channels)). The survivors moved to multi-agent support (Forge Remote, Tactic Remote) or to an external system of record. Two details favour this slice specifically: Channels covers Telegram, Discord and iMessage but **not Slack or Teams**, and Anthropic's direction is removing prompts (auto mode, routines without approval) rather than routing them, which makes a first-party async Slack approver plausible but unsignalled. One note's claim that Team and Enterprise plans were excluded from Remote Control is stale; the current docs list all four paid plans with an owner toggle on Team and Enterprise, so the product cannot be positioned as "Remote Control for teams" and must be positioned as "approval when nobody is at the terminal".

## Two weeks: verify the cloud hook, ship the installer, count paying installs

Build first the narrowest thing that uses every existing asset: a one-line `npx decidio init` that writes a Claude Code `PreToolUse` hook with `onFailure: "block"` plus a `PermissionRequest` hook (allow/deny only, since exit code 2 is not honoured there), installs a default destructive-operations policy, and routes anything the policy flags to the existing Slack, Teams, phone and web inbox, with the signed receipt URL written back through `PostToolUse` so the transcript proves the gate ran. The default policy should cover exactly the incident classes with the blast radius shown in the approval card: MCP database writes (`DELETE`, `DROP`, `TRUNCATE`, `UPDATE` without a `WHERE`, or any write above a row threshold), `git push --force`, `rm -rf` outside the working tree, `terraform destroy` and `apply`, and any tool call in a session where `$CLAUDE_CODE_REMOTE` is set. A one-time grant with a long expiry answers the "timeout is not consent" complaint directly, and the MCP tool annotations already shipped let the same policy fire for ChatGPT and Claude.ai connector writes. Add a Codex adapter in the same sprint because Codex gained a blocking `PermissionRequest` hook in April 2026 ([openai/codex #15311](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/15311)) but still errors on `permissionDecision: ask` ([#28437](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/28437)), so the adapter must hold the call itself and return allow or deny; Gemini CLI's `BeforeTool` can only deny or rewrite ([gemini-cli hooks](https://github.com/google-gemini/gemini-cli/blob/main/docs/hooks/index.md)) and can wait.

Days one and two go to the single unknown that could invalidate the headline: commit an `http` hook pointing at the gate API to a test repository's `.claude/settings.json`, start a cloud session and a routine, trigger a flagged action, and record whether the hook fires, whether the session waits, and whether it expires while waiting (the cloud docs say a session "counts as inactive while it waits for you to approve an MCP connector tool call" and can expire ([Cloud docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-code-on-the-web))). If cloud works, that is the launch headline, because nobody sells it. If it does not, the headline becomes "gated scheduled runs and database writes on your own machine", which still has the 39-and-43-count issues and the 55k-row incident behind it, and the cloud finding becomes a documented limitation rather than a hidden one. Days three to five ship the installer, the pricing page through the merchant of record, and a plain security statement that copies Pushary's own honesty ("a strong guardrail, not a security boundary") and lists the known hook blind spots (subagents, hook timeouts, Codex desktop updates) rather than hiding them, because the #15922 thread shows the main objection to any third party is "I don't trust any third party apps with no real reputation" ([#15922](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/15922)).

Launch on day five or six into the places where the pain is already written down: r/ClaudeAI and r/ChatGPTCoding posts framed around the 55k-row and `terraform destroy` incidents, a single informative comment on #13325, #44771, #23200, #28437 and #28969 (these threads are where the exact asks live and a maintainer-abandoned issue is a legitimate place to say "this exists"), a listing in the community Claude plugin marketplace, and a Show HN in week two for the backlink rather than for customers. Instrument four numbers from the first install: installs, hooks that fired at least one real approval, approvals delivered per channel, and conversions to any paid tier at any price. The decision thresholds at day fourteen are judgment calls anchored on the benchmarks: **at least 100 installs, at least 30% of installs producing a real approval event, and at least five paying customers at any price** is a "pull" signal strong enough to keep building; fewer than 25 installs or zero payers after the Reddit and issue posts means the slice is starred-not-paid and the deploy gate moves up. The first ten payers get a fifteen-minute call with three questions: which action triggered the approval, who approved it (self or someone else), and whether they would pay for the receipt alone. The answer to the second question decides whether the second-person approver becomes the next tier. In parallel and at near-zero cost, put up a one-page waitlist for "deploy approvals for GitHub Team private repos, no runner minutes, Slack approval, signed record" and link it from the same posts; a few dozen signups in two weeks is enough to justify the GitHub App build, and none is enough to park it.

## Avoid phone approvals, PII compliance, context packs and enterprise audit

Do not lead with approving your own prompt from your phone. It has the strongest "people asking" signal of anything researched (#15922, Codex #9224 with a 52-thumbs comment, ten-plus Telegram clones) and is the one place people demonstrably pay, but both vendors now ship it inside the subscription, Happy offers it free and open source at 24.2k stars ([slopus/happy](https://github.com/slopus/happy)), and the best-funded indie in the category, Omnara, pivoted away from mobile approvals to a managed-agent platform ([omnara-ai/omnara](https://github.com/omnara-ai/omnara)). The same logic rules out Bash destructive-command blocking as a product: dcg and cc-safety-net have 20–80 times the stars of any remote-approval hook, the vendors' deny rules "block in every mode, including bypassPermissions" ([Permission modes](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permission-modes)), and nobody pays for a guard. Both belong inside the product as free features that make the install worth doing, not as the thing being sold.

Do not sell the PII vault to lawyers, accountants or therapists first, even though the vault is a finished asset and professional guidance is loud. The guidance (ABA Formal Opinion 512's informed-consent requirement, ICAEW's "potential breach" language) is tool-agnostic and pushes responsibility onto the professional ([ABA Law Practice](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/law_practice/resources/law-practice-magazine/2026/march-april-2026/educate-clients-about-confidentiality-risks-of-genai-tools); [ICAEW](https://www.icaew.com/regulation/regulatory-news/regulatory-news-2024-02/dont-let-ai-tools-land-you-in-hot-water)), which means every sale carries an implied compliance promise the founder must then disclaim, and pseudonymised data "can remain personal data" under GDPR while HIPAA de-identification is a strict standard that session narratives rarely meet ([Wavect](https://wavect.io/blog/pii-redaction-before-llm-prompts.md); [Telehealth.org](https://telehealth.org/blog/chatgpt-hipaa/)). The supply side is fifteen-plus free extensions with user counts in the tens (PromptSafe at 22 users per an aggregator, ChatGPT Privacy Shield at 1–8), the only observed prices are **$19–29 one-time** with conflicting aggregator figures ([Chromeboard](https://www.chromeboard.com/extension/promptsafe-pii-protection-fdhopggddjbjkakmidmpmnegboffooon); [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/all-software/a/promptshield/)), and money flows instead to substitutes that are "a compliant place to put the data": ChatGPT Business seats at $20–25 and BAA-backed wrappers from $18.99/month. All extension user counts and prices in that note come from aggregator snippets because the stores were unreachable. The developer secrets variant is closing too, since dotenvx (5.8k stars) already redacts secrets from Claude, Codex and Cursor output for free ([dotenvx](https://github.com/dotenvx/dotenvx)) and Gemini CLI ships `environmentVariableRedaction` natively. The one open vault slice, tokenizing customer rows in agent context with role tokens and an audited restore, has three GitHub issues with zero reactions behind it and is demand that would have to be created.

Do not sell context packs or sealed decision memory. The context note's own conclusion is that nobody asks for "a curated, trigger-phrased pack of sealed decisions" in any words, that trigger-phrased routing is table stakes the platforms already give away, that free OSS has absorbed rule sync (Ruler ~3k stars, rulesync ~1.5k, claude-mem 101k) ([intellectronica/ruler](https://gittrend.io/repo/intellectronica/ruler)), that the loudest pain, rules being ignored, is a model-attention problem no context product can fix ("Claude treats CLAUDE.md files as context, not enforced configuration" ([memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory))), and that the nearest commercial entrants (ContextStream at $79/user, Tandem, Hjarni, BaseThread) publish no traction at all ([ContextStream review](https://vibecoding.app/blog/contextstream-review)). Decision-log tools built for agents sit at 4 and 35 stars. The packs remain useful as free onboarding content for the gate product's policies, nothing more.

Do not pitch the tamper-evident audit trail to teams or security buyers, and do not build for OpenClaw first. The audit demand is real (the Cursor forum request that approval decisions are "not persisted in any queryable, exportable form", Codex's "audit receipts" ask) but the buyer is a platform or security team, which is a sales cycle, and the only open-source project that built exactly the Slack-approver-plus-signed-log design has zero stars after 277 commits ([agent-governance-plane](https://github.com/jeremylongshore/agent-governance-plane)), which is weak evidence that developers will pull it bottom-up. OpenClaw is the largest install base with the weakest native story, since its approvals cover shell `exec` only and the fallback is deny-when-no-UI ([OpenClaw exec approvals](https://docs.openclaw.ai/tools/exec-approvals)), and the Yue incident is the cleanest public demonstration that a prompt-level "confirm before acting" is not a control because it was lost in context compaction ([AI Incident Database](https://incidentdatabase.ai/entities/summer-yue/)). But its plugin API was never assessed, the issue closure reasons were never read, its users are self-hosters with a free-software culture, and non-developer willingness to pay for a standalone guardrail is unproven in every source. It is the right third adapter after Codex, sold with the same `request_approval` tool and the Yue story as the headline example, not the first product. Finally, do not open-source the core or offer a generous free tier in the hope of stars: Vibe Kanban, Happy and ccusage show that the most-starred tools in this category earned nothing, and Vercel's own Slack deploy-approval template has one star ([chat-sdk-deploy-bot](https://github.com/vercel-labs/chat-sdk-deploy-bot)).

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. The founder's assets are not a product looking for a market; they are a complete answer to a specific sentence that appears, in slightly different words, in closed issues at both vendors: "pause the agent, ask a human who is not at the keyboard, and prove afterwards what was approved". Vendors have declined to build that because their roadmap is fewer prompts, and the free ecosystem has not built it because the hobby projects that tried (Pylon, agent-governance-plane, repowire) stop at notification or at a local log. That leaves a narrow, defensible, developer-paid wedge whose main risk is not competition but the possibility that developers want the agent to simply not do the dangerous thing (a free guard) rather than to ask (a paid gate). The two-week test is designed to settle exactly that, by counting whether people who install the free guard pay for the routed, receipted ask.

Two implications follow for what comes after. If the cloud-session hook works, the product's strongest claim becomes something no first-party feature touches: a human gate on runs that start from a schedule, a Slack message or an API call, which is the direction every vendor is pushing usage. And the deploy gate, the OpenClaw adapter and the second-person approver are not separate products but the same receipt attached to three more triggers, so each one should be added only when the previous one's payers ask for it in the exit interview, never on the strength of a star count.
